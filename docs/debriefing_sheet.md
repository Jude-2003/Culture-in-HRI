## What was this study about?
 
This study is investigating whether cultural background influences how people respond to different robot communication styles. Specifically, we are looking at two cultural dimensions that have been extensively studied in cross-cultural psychology: **Power Distance**, how comfortable people are with authority and hierarchical relationships, and **Uncertainty Avoidance**, how much people prefer predictability and structure over ambiguity.
 
Participants from the United Kingdom and Saudi Arabia were recruited because these two countries show one of the largest contrasts on both of these dimensions globally.
 
---
 
## What condition were you in?
 
In the **City Builder task**, the robot either communicated in a directive, authoritative style or a peer-like, collaborative style. This was designed to test whether cultural background predicts how comfortable people are with each style.
 
In the **Sorting Task**, the robot either gave clear, consistent guidance or more variable, open-ended guidance. This was designed to test whether cultural background predicts how people respond to predictable versus uncertain robot behaviour.
 
You were randomly assigned to one of these conditions. Both styles are equally valid; neither is better or worse than the other. The robot's communication style was the experimental manipulation, not a reflection of your performance or decisions.
 
---
 
## Why does this matter?
 
Robots are increasingly being deployed in everyday settings, in hospitals, schools, and public services, across the world. Most robot design has been based on research conducted with Western participants. This study aims to contribute to a better understanding of how robots should communicate across different cultural contexts, so that they can work effectively and appropriately for everyone.
